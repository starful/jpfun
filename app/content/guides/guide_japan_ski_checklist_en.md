**New to skiing in Japan?** This checklist is built for exactly that — the small logistics (passes, rental, insurance, cash) that trip up first-timers most. Work through it in order and you'll land in Niseko or Hakuba ready to ride, not scrambling.

Your first Japan ski trip goes smoother when the boring logistics are done before you land. Use this checklist for **Hokkaido (Niseko, Rusutsu, Furano)** or **Honshu (Hakuba, Nozawa, Yuzawa)** — the details change by resort, but the sequence is the same.

JPFun maps resorts with curated **Stay** and **Food** pins so you can lock lodging and meals after you pick a mountain.

## 4–8 weeks before departure

- **Pick a region:** Powder priority → Hokkaido; Tokyo day-trip or Olympic-scale runs → Hakuba / Yuzawa.
- **Book lodging early** for late Dec – mid-Feb and Lunar New Year. Slope-side sells out first.
- **Flights:** Seoul → New Chitose (CTS) for Hokkaido; Narita/Haneda for Hakuba or Gala Yuzawa.
- **Travel insurance:** Confirm winter sports are covered (slope skiing/snowboarding). Standard tourist policies often exclude snow sports — read the fine print.
- **Passport & entry:** Check current Japan entry rules for your nationality; keep digital + paper copies of bookings.

## Lift passes & lessons

- Compare **single-resort vs. multi-area** tickets (Niseko United, Hakuba Valley). Multi-day passes usually beat day tickets after 3+ days.
- Buy online when the resort discounts advance purchase — print or save QR codes offline.
- Book **English or Korean lessons** for kids or first-timers; peak-week slots fill early.
- Confirm which lifts open on your dates (early/late season varies by mountain).

## Rentals & packing

- **Rent on mountain** if you want to travel light; reserve boots/boards online for busy weeks.
- **Bring:** waterproof layers, goggles (low-light lens for cloudy powder days), helmet, gloves ×2, sunscreen, lip balm.
- **Boards/skis as checked luggage:** check airline size/weight fees; many travelers use luggage forwarding from airport to hotel in Japan.
- Label gear bags with hotel address in Japanese if possible.

## Money & phones

- Carry some **cash (JPY)** for small shops and rural buses; major resorts take cards, but not every izakaya does.
- IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) help in Tokyo; Hokkaido local transport is more cash/ticket-based.
- Pocket Wi‑Fi or eSIM before arrival — map apps and lift status pages matter on storm days.

## Arrival week checklist

1. Confirm **shuttle pickup** time from airport or station.
2. Check **live lift & weather** the night before your first ski day.
3. Rent gear the evening you arrive if possible — shorter queues than 8 a.m. powder mornings.
4. Save JPFun **Stay / Food** pins for après without scrolling random maps.
5. Know your resort’s **lost & found / patrol** location.

## Common first-trip mistakes

- Buying only a day pass when you ski 4+ days.
- Underestimating **cold + wind** on Hokkaido powder days.
- Booking Hirafu nightlife lodging when you actually want quiet Annupuri or Rusutsu.
- Skipping insurance “because I’m careful.”
