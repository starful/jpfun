**The trade-off, upfront:** Chiba costs extra train time, but it buys you quieter peaks on the weekends Shonan turns into a zoo or a mush-fest. This is the 2-night version of that trade — pick a coast, commit to it, and don't burn the trip chasing three different towns.

**Chiba** rewards the extra train time when Shonan is mushy or overrun.

Pin breaks on JPFun **Surf · Kanto**, then commit to Chiba lodging — do not bounce back to Shonan mid-trip.

## Day 0 — Move east

- Arrive with daylight for a short recon paddle if the window is open.
- Rent locally; ask which peak is working tomorrow AM.

## Day 1 — Main sessions

- Morning priority. Afternoon only if wind stays friendly.
- Two focused sessions beat chasing three different towns.

## Day 2 — Last window + return

- Early surf, then Tokyo with buffer.
- Flat: food day ([okramen.net](https://okramen.net/)) instead of forcing closeouts.

## Route rule

Chiba **or** Shonan — one coast per weekend.

## Who This Trip Suits

Good for surfers based in Tokyo who want real space in the water without hopping regions mid-trip. If your plan is checking several breaks in one weekend, Shonan's cross-region bouncing still wins — this Chiba route is for committing to one coast and riding it out.

**Practical tips**

- Check the Kanto swell/wind forecast the night before Day 0 — it decides which Chiba town you base in.
- Pack a spring suit and a layer; Chiba mornings run cooler than Shonan.
- Travel light — you're surfing two mornings, not touring three towns.

## Getting to Chiba From Tokyo

Most of this route starts with a train out of central Tokyo toward the Chiba coast — budget extra transfer time versus a straight shot to Shonan, and build it into your Day 0 window instead of treating it as dead time. Once you're off the train, a short local rental or taxi hop gets you to the beach; ask locally which peak is working before you commit. If you're weighing Chiba against Shonan for a weekend trip, the extra travel is the point — it's what keeps these peaks quieter.
