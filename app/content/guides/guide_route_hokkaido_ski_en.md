A short **Hokkaido ski route** works best when you pick **one village base**, ski hard for two days, and keep one soft day for weather or recovery.

Open the JPFun **Ski · Hokkaido** map first, then pin 2–3 resorts within a short drive of your stay.

## Day 0 — Arrive & settle

- Fly into New Chitose; bus or train toward **Niseko** (international feel, tree runs) or **Furano** (calmer, big-mountain views).
- Check gear rental hours; reserve if you land late.
- Early night — powder days start early.

## Days 1–2 — Ski the base

- Ski the home mountain first; learn lift lines and snow reports before day-tripping.
- Keep a second resort on the JPFun map as a backup if wind closes upper lifts.
- Lunch near the base; avoid over-planning restaurants until you know the snow day length.

## Day 3 — Flex day

- **Great snow:** another full ski day or a short hop to a nearby hill.
- **Whiteout / legs done:** onsen town soak — browse [okonsen.net](https://okonsen.net/) for a ryokan or day bath on the return corridor.
- **Need carbs:** a ramen stop via [okramen.net](https://okramen.net/).

## Day 4 — Exit buffer

- Morning ski only if flights are late; otherwise transfer with margin for winter roads.
- Screenshot your JPFun shortlist before you lose signal in the hills.

## Packing anchors

- Layering for wet cold, goggle spare lens, cash for some mountain vendors.
- Travel insurance that covers off-piste if you plan to leave marked runs.

This route is intentionally thin: **base + powder + one recovery pin**. Add Hakuba later on a second trip — do not merge both into one first visit.
