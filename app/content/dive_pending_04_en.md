Iriomote sits at the far southwestern edge of Japan, closer to Taipei than to Tokyo, and the diving reflects that geography. Mangrove rivers spill nutrient-rich water into a fringing reef system that's stayed remarkably intact compared to the more heavily dived corners of Okinawa Prefecture. For anyone chasing a quieter, wilder version of Japanese diving, Iriomote is the answer.

## Who Comes to Iriomote and Why It's Not Like the Rest of Okinawa

Iriomote attracts a specific kind of diver: people who've already done Ishigaki or Kerama and want something less trafficked. About 90% of the island is covered in subtropical jungle and protected as Iriomote-Ishigaki National Park, and that same protection extends into the surrounding waters. There's no mass-market dive strip here — no rows of shops competing for walk-ins. Most operators run small boats out of Uehara Port or Ohara Port, capping groups at four to six divers.

This is also one of the few places in Japan where you can combine world-class reef diving with jungle trekking, kayaking through mangrove tunnels, and waterfall hikes in the same trip. Divers who want a single-purpose dive resort should look elsewhere; Iriomote rewards people happy to spend surface intervals exploring Pinaisara Falls or paddling the Nakama River. Families with older teens, underwater photographers chasing macro subjects in the mangrove estuaries, and manta-ray specialists heading to nearby Kohama Island all end up here for the same reason — the ecosystem above water is as intact as the one below it.

Because the island has no major resort development, expect a slower pace: small guesthouses, home-cooked Okinawan meals, and dive briefings that often double as marine biology lessons from guides who've worked these reefs for decades.

## Dive Site Conditions: Reef Walls, Manta Points, and Mangrove Swim-Throughs

Iriomote's dive sites split roughly into three zones, and a good local operator will move between them depending on wind direction and swell.

**Barasu Island and the surrounding reef flats** offer some of the calmest, most beginner-friendly coral gardens in the area, with staghorn coral fields in 5–12 meters that are ideal for check-out dives or photography-focused sessions. Visibility here typically runs 15–20 meters.

**The outer reef walls off the southern coast** drop from 8 meters down past 30, with overhangs, small caves, and a resident population of reef sharks and turtles. Current can pick up here, especially around new and full moon tides, so operators usually reserve these sites for certified divers with at least a handful of logged dives.

**Kohama Kaigan (the channel between Iriomote and Kohama Island)** is the region's marquee manta cleaning station. From roughly May through November, reef manta rays gather here to be cleaned by wrasse, and multi-manta encounters are common on good days. This site sits in open water with real current, so it's typically run as a drift dive with a live boat, and shops will usually ask for 20+ logged dives or an Advanced certification before booking you on it.

Water temperature ranges from around 23°C in winter (a 5mm wetsuit is comfortable) to 29–30°C in summer, when a 3mm or rashguard is enough. Visibility across the region averages 15–25 meters outside of typhoon season, dropping noticeably after heavy rain due to runoff from the island's rivers.

## Season and Access: When to Dive and How to Actually Get There

**Best season:** April through November is the core diving window, with the manta season (May–November) as the headline draw. June through September brings the warmest water and calmest seas but also the highest typhoon risk — it's worth building a flexible itinerary with a spare day or two, since boats cancel outright when a storm system is nearby. Winter (December–March) is diveable with a wetsuit and thinner crowds, though manta encounters drop off and wind makes outer-reef sites less reliable.

**Getting there:** There's no airport on Iriomote itself. The route runs through Naha (Okinawa's main hub) — fly into Naha Airport, then connect to Ishigaki Airport (roughly 1 hour by air, several flights daily on ANA, JTA, and Peach). From Ishigaki, high-speed ferries run to Iriomote's two ports, Uehara (north side, ~40–50 minutes) and Ohara (south side, ~35–40 minutes), operated by Yaeyama Kanko Ferry and Anei Kanko. Ferries run roughly hourly during peak season but are among the first services cancelled in rough weather, so don't schedule a same-day connection back to a flight home if you can avoid it.

Once on Iriomote, a rental car or your dive shop's pickup van is the practical way to get around — public transport is limited to a single infrequent bus route along the coastal road. Most dive shops offer port or accommodation pickup, which is worth using if you're not renting a car, since accommodation is spread thinly along the north and south coasts.

## Practical Tips for Booking, Gear, and Making the Most of the Island

**Booking:** Reserve at least a few weeks ahead for peak season (July–August, and Golden Week in late April/early May), especially if you specifically want the Kohama manta site — shops often run it only when conditions allow and will prioritize divers who've pre-booked that itinerary. Many shops communicate primarily in Japanese; booking through an English-friendly operator or a booking platform that handles translation will save friction.

**Gear:** Bring your own mask and, if you have one, a compact travel BCD — rental gear availability is more limited than on Ishigaki or the mainland, and sizing options can be narrow. A dive computer is expected rather than optional at most shops. If you're diving the manta channel or outer walls, a hood and gloves aren't required by regulation but help against the odd bit of current-driven chill even in summer.

**Etiquette and conservation:** Iriomote's reefs are protected partly because divers here are strict about buoyancy and no-touch practices — gloves are discouraged at several sites specifically to prevent people bracing on coral. Reef-safe sunscreen is a genuine expectation, not a suggestion, given how much of the surrounding water feeds into the national park's protected zones.

**Stay and food:** Base yourself near Uehara if you want easy access to the manta sites and northern jungle trails, or near Ohara if you're prioritizing the southern outer reefs and a shorter ferry connection. Guesthouses typically include breakfast and can arrange dinner featuring local specialties like yaeyama soba and fresh-caught fish; very few places take walk-ins during peak months, so book lodging alongside your dive schedule rather than after it.

**Timing your trip:** Pair two or three days of diving with at least one full day inland — the Nakama River mangrove kayak trip and the hike to Pinaisara Falls are both easy to combine with an afternoon dive, and give you the fuller Iriomote experience that separates this island from a standard Okinawa dive trip.
