## A Current-Powered Dive for Divers Who Want Movement, Not Just a View

Sodefuri Island sits off the Ehime coastline in a stretch of water where the warm Kuroshio influence pushes just far enough north to keep the reef surprisingly tropical for mainland Japan. The dive here is built entirely around drift: you drop in up-current, let the water do the work, and glide along a wall and boulder field thick with soft coral, anemone clusters, and reef fish that behave more like Okinawa residents than Shikoku locals. This is not a site for divers who want to hover motionless over a single coral head for forty minutes — it rewards people who like covering ground, reading the water, and trusting a dive guide's drop timing.

It suits intermediate to advanced certified divers comfortable with moderate current, negative entries, and mid-water safety stops without a fixed line. Open Water divers with limited drift experience can still do it on a calm tide window with a guide close by, but this is better described as a site that builds drift confidence than one for a first-ever certification dive. Underwater photographers like it for the wide-angle reef-and-current compositions; fish-watchers like it because the current concentrates schooling species against the lee side of boulders, turning a short dive into a steady parade of activity instead of a slow search.

What makes Sodefuri distinct from other Ehime dive spots is the combination: Seto Inland Sea access logistics with genuinely tropical-leaning marine life and a real, current-driven drift profile rather than a lazy inshore drift. Divers who already know Shikoku's cooler, muckier bay sites are usually surprised by how clear and colorful this one runs on a good tide.

## Reading the Reefs: Sites, Depths, and the Drift That Defines Sodefuri

The dive area wraps the southern and eastern flanks of Sodefuri Island, where a rocky spine drops from the shallows into a boulder-strewn slope bottoming out between 18 and 28 meters. Most drift runs start at the upstream point of the island, where boats time a negative entry to catch the main current line, then follow it along the wall as it sweeps divers past a series of distinct terrain features: a swim-through crevice around 12 meters, a soft coral-draped overhang near 20 meters, and a cleaning station on a mid-slope boulder where local wrasse service passing fish.

Visibility runs 8 to 20 meters depending on tide and recent rain runoff from the mainland, typically clearest on the stronger flood tides when offshore water pushes in rather than inshore water pushing out. Water temperature swings widely by season — expect the low-to-mid 20s°C (Celsius) in peak summer and autumn, dropping into the high teens by late winter, which is why most operators treat this as a spring-through-autumn site and run it in a 5mm wetsuit or drysuit outside the warm months.

Marine life skews reef-tropical: parrotfish, butterflyfish, damselfish, and occasional turtle sightings along the wall, with larger pelagics — amberjack, occasionally small tuna — using the current line itself as a feeding corridor. Current strength is tide-dependent and can run from a gentle 0.5-knot cruise to a brisker 1.5+ knots around peak tidal exchange, which is exactly why dive shops schedule around tide tables rather than a fixed daily time slot. Guides typically brief an entry point, an exit point, and a bailout plan if the group gets split by current — standard drift-dive protocol, but worth confirming before you back-roll in.

## Timing the Tides: Best Months and the Route in From Matsuyama

The dive season at Sodefuri runs roughly from late April through early November, with the best combination of warm water, good visibility, and calm surface conditions falling in the July-to-October window. Typhoon season (August through October) can cancel trips with little notice, so building a buffer day or two into a dive trip itinerary is worth doing rather than booking a single tight dive day during a visit to Ehime.

Access starts at Matsuyama Airport, the main air gateway into Ehime, with direct flights from Tokyo (Haneda), Osaka, and Fukuoka. From Matsuyama Airport, it's roughly 15 to 20 minutes by taxi or rental car to central Matsuyama, and from there the drive down toward the dive departure port on Ehime's southern coast takes another 90 minutes to 2 hours depending on the exact launch point, mostly via the Matsuyama Expressway and coastal roads. Renting a car at the airport is the most practical option for most divers, since dive boats generally depart from small fishing ports without convenient train access.

For those without a car, Matsuyama Station connects by limited express JR trains to Uwajima and other southern Ehime towns, from which local dive shops can often arrange pickup if booked in advance — but this adds transfer time and should be confirmed directly with the operator rather than assumed. Many dive shops running Sodefuri trips offer hotel pickup from Matsuyama city itself, which is the simplest option if you're not driving. Total door-to-boat time from Matsuyama Airport typically lands between 2 and 2.5 hours, so plan an early flight if you want to dive the same day you land, or build in an overnight in Matsuyama before heading to the coast.

## Diving Smart: Booking, Gear, and Reef Etiquette Around Sodefuri

Book through an Ehime-based dive shop rather than trying to arrange boat access independently — local operators know the tide windows that make or break a drift dive at Sodefuri, and most run small-group charters (typically 4 to 8 divers) rather than large dive-boat operations. Reserve at least a few days ahead in peak season (July through September), and earlier if you want a specific weekend slot, since good tide days fill fast. Certification proof (Open Water minimum, Advanced Open Water or drift-specialty preferred) and recent dive log are standard requirements; some shops require a guided check dive first if you haven't dived with them before.

Gear-wise, a 3mm to 5mm wetsuit covers most of the warm season, with hooded vests or thicker suits recommended outside July–September. A surface marker buoy (SMB) is close to mandatory on drift dives here — boats track divers by SMB and current direction, and shops will often lend one if you don't carry your own. A reef hook is useful but less essential than at stronger-current sites, since Sodefuri's flow is manageable for most certified divers; ask your guide whether conditions that day warrant one.

Reef etiquette matters more than usual on a drift dive, since the current makes accidental fin contact with coral and rock easy if buoyancy isn't dialed in — keep hands and fins off the substrate, maintain a slight positive trim relative to the slope, and resist the urge to grab rock to "pause" against current; let the drift carry you and use your guide's pacing instead. No touching marine life, no collecting shells or coral, and pack out anything you bring in, including used gloves or hood liners.

For food and stay, most divers base themselves in Matsuyama the night before and after a Sodefuri trip, where the city's hot-spring baths (Dogo Onsen is a short detour inland) make a genuinely good post-dive recovery stop, and the local specialty — Imabari yakibuta tamagomeshi or fresh Seto Inland Sea seafood — is worth seeking out over a generic hotel dinner. Smaller coastal towns near the dive departure port usually have simple minshuku guesthouses and seafood diners catering directly to the dive crowd, often run by families connected to the dive shop itself, which makes for an easier, more local alternative to commuting from the city each day.
